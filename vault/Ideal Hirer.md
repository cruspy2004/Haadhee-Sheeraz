# Ideal Hirer

One avatar. Not a segment, not two lanes — one person, with a name, a
calendar and a bad week. Everything on the site is built for him or it is
cut. → [[Positioning]]

## Why one and not two

[[Start Here]] says two lanes is the maximum a portfolio can carry. That
was right for the evidence and wrong for the site. A page built for two
readers is built for neither: it hedges, it lists, it explains itself
twice.

Both lanes collapse into one person anyway. At a 9-person company the
"product engineer" and the "developer advocate" are the same hire,
because there is nobody else. That person is the avatar.

## Daniel

**Technical co-founder / CTO. 9 people. Seed. Remote-first.**

- 31. Was a senior engineer at a real company, left to build this.
- Product is an API or a devtool. **Their customer is a developer.**
  AI infra, auth, payments, data, CI — the category matters less than the
  fact that the buyer reads docs.
- Raised $2–4M eighteen months ago. Roughly 20 months of runway left and
  he checks that number more often than he admits.
- Team: 4 engineers, 1 designer, 2 GTM, 2 founders. **No recruiter. No HR.
  No marketing team.** He is the hiring process.
- Writes code most days. Has 200 unread Slack messages right now.

### What he is hiring for

Not a headcount plan. A specific hole:

> "We ship fine. Nobody knows we exist. I can't afford a marketing hire
> and every DevRel candidate I've met can't code."

He would take one person who does both halves at 70% over two people who
do one half at 100%, because two people is a budget he does not have.

### What he can actually offer you

Be honest about this, it defines everything:

- Remote contract or internship, **$1.5k–4k/month**. Not a US salary.
- No visa sponsorship. He is not going to relocate a 3rd-year student.
- No degree requirement. He genuinely does not care about NUST.
- Start next month, part-time, prove it, convert. That is the real path
  from where you are: student until Aug 2027, already at [[FlyRank AI]].

### How he actually finds people

Never a job board. He posts once on X or LinkedIn, gets 300 replies in
48 hours, and skims them on his phone between meetings.

**Your portfolio gets 40 seconds on a phone.** If it survives, it gets
3 minutes on a laptop that evening. There is no third round of attention
and no committee — one man, one sitting, one decision.

## His pain, ranked

Ranked by how much it hurts him, not by how easy it is for you to answer.

### 1. Everyone looks identical and he cannot tell who actually ships

300 replies. 290 are a to-do app, a weather app, a Figma-pretty landing
page and the phrase "passionate about clean code". He has no way to sort
them and no time to try.

**The fear underneath:** spending twenty minutes to learn nothing.

**What kills it:** one specific, strange, checkable number in the first
five seconds. *5,000 employees.* *3M views.* Numbers that a bootcamp
graduate could not have.

### 2. He has been lied to before

He once hired someone who could not explain their own project in the
second call. Claims on a junior CV are, to him, unverified by default.

**The fear underneath:** being fooled, again, in front of his co-founder.

**What kills it:** things he can click and check *without talking to you*
— a live URL, a repo he can read, a public account whose follower count
cannot be faked. → [[Gaps]] #1, the Watify write-up, is this exact hole.

### 3. A junior costs him more time than they save

His hours are the scarcest resource in the company. A hire who needs a
week of onboarding, asks what a Google search answers, and stalls the
moment a task crosses from backend to frontend is **net-negative for
three months.** He has done that once and it nearly broke him.

**The fear underneath:** becoming a babysitter instead of a builder.

**What kills it:** evidence of *unsupervised end-to-end ownership.*
[[Watify]] answers it. [[H1GROW]] answers it. What answers it best is any
sign you shipped something with nobody checking your work.

This is the biggest pain and almost no junior portfolio addresses it,
because juniors think the question is "am I good enough" when the
question is "what will this cost me".

### 4. Nobody knows his product exists

He wrote three blog posts. Twelve people read them. He posted on HN once
and it sank. Distribution is the thing standing between him and the next
round, and he cannot buy it: a DevRel hire is $120k and most of them
cannot code.

**The fear underneath:** dying with a good product.

**What kills it:** [[JavascriptPeter]]. 0 → 7.5k in two months, in his
exact audience — JavaScript developers — with a **pipeline**, which means
repeatable rather than lucky. To him that is not a side project. It is
the second half of the hire he could not afford.

### 5. Will you still be here in six months

Student. Exams. Another offer. He has been ghosted by a part-timer
mid-sprint and had to rewrite their branch himself.

**The fear underneath:** running this whole search again in January.

**What kills it:** continuity he can see — currently shipping at
[[FlyRank AI]], multi-year threads, and one honest line about how many
hours a week you actually have. Honesty here reads as low-risk, not as
weakness.

### 6. Every hour of translation is an hour he does not have

Distributed team, 8-hour timezone gap, everything async. Someone who
writes vaguely turns into a standing call.

**The fear underneath:** more meetings.

**What kills it:** the portfolio itself. He is not reading it for content,
he is reading it as a **writing sample.** Every sentence on the site is
the interview for this one.

### 7. Can you read code you did not write

Day one is not a greenfield repo. It is 40k lines someone else wrote,
badly, at 2am, before Series A.

**Nothing on the site answers this.** Already flagged → [[Gaps]] #3.
One merged PR to a project he has heard of would close it permanently.

## The one job

> **In 60 seconds, make Daniel believe that Haadhee ships production
> things alone and can put a product in front of developers — and let him
> verify both himself, without messaging anyone.**

That is the whole brief. It is one sentence because the site is allowed
one job.

Two claims. Two proofs. Both checkable.

| Claim | Proof | Checkable how |
|---|---|---|
| Ships production things alone | [[Watify]] — 5,000 users | live URL + repo + write-up |
| Puts products in front of developers | [[JavascriptPeter]] — 3M views | public account, public numbers |

Everything else on the site is either **supporting** those two or it is
**noise**, however good it was. [[Memetent]] is noise. The skills list is
noise. The degree is noise. → [[Gaps]] #2

## The cut test

For every element on the page, one question:

> Does this help Daniel believe one of the two claims, in under 60
> seconds, without asking anyone?

Not "is it true", not "is it impressive", not "did it take a long time".
If the answer is no, it is spending his attention on something that is
not the sale.

## Who this deliberately loses

Naming them is the point. A position that repels nobody is not a position.

- **Enterprise and big-tech recruiters.** They screen for keywords and
  GPA. The 35-technology list was written for them. → [[Gaps]] #2
- **Agencies hiring a pure frontend.** Nothing on the site is UI work.
- **Anyone who needs a specialist.** The range is the product here; to
  them it reads unfocused. Let it.

Losing these is a decision, not an accident.
